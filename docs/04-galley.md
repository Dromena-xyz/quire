# Galley

Breaking a long work into scene notes gives you control: you can reorder,
outline, and reason about the parts. But prose is read and written in flow, not
in fragments, and editing scene by scene can leave you stitching the seams in
your head. A Galley removes that tradeoff.

A Galley stitches any run of scenes into one continuous document you can read and
write as a whole, while every keystroke flows back to the individual scene notes
underneath. This is the piece writers reach for most.

## Write the Whole Thing

Open a Galley on the scenes you want:

- From a scene's menu, choose **Open in Galley** to open that scene together with
  its children as one document.
- Or select several scenes in the list and open the selection as one Galley.

Quire assembles the chosen scenes in order, with a quiet divider marking each
boundary. Write anywhere in the document and your edits save back into the
matching scene note as you go. There is no separate save step and no second copy
of your prose to reconcile: the scene notes remain the source of truth, and the
Galley is a live view onto them. Drafting in flow, revising across a scene
boundary, reading a chapter for pace: it is all the same continuous surface.

## Scenes Step Aside While a Galley Is Open

A Galley takes exclusive charge of its scenes so you never edit the same prose in
two places at once. When you open one, Quire closes any standalone tabs showing
those scenes; when you close the Galley, it reopens them where they were. Edit in
the Galley while it is open, and your individual scene tabs come back the moment
you are done.

## Galley and Compile

A Galley is for working: a live, editable view you read and revise in. When you
want a finished, exported document instead, with formatting steps applied, use
[Compile](05-compile.md).
