# Galley

A Galley stitches several of a project's scenes into one document so you can read
or revise them as a continuous whole, then writes every change back to the
individual scene files. It is the fastest way to edit across scene boundaries
without losing the structure you built.

## Open a Galley

Select the scenes you want in the scene list and open them as a Galley, or open a
single scene and its subtree from its menu. Quire assembles the chosen scenes, in
order, into one editable document.

Between scenes you will see a divider marking each boundary. The text under a
divider belongs to that scene.

## Editing flows back to the files

Type anywhere in the Galley and your edits sync back to the matching scene file,
continuously. There is no separate save step and no second copy of your prose to
keep in sync: the scene files remain the source of truth, and the Galley is a
live view onto them.

Because a Galley takes ownership of its scenes while it is open, edit them there
rather than in a separate tab at the same time. When you close the Galley, any
scene tabs it set aside reopen where you left them.

## When to use it

- Reading a run of scenes end to end to check flow and pacing.
- Revising across a boundary, moving a paragraph from the end of one scene into
  the start of the next.
- Light restructuring where you want the prose continuous but the files intact.

For a single combined export rather than live editing, use [Compile](compile.md).
